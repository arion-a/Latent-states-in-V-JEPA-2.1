1Definitions, dimensions & scene assignment

Let $S = \{0,\dots,39\}$, $Q \subset S$ the 20 color-control scene IDs, $T \subset S$ the 30 training IDs, and $U = S \setminus T$ the 10 test IDs. All sets are fixed before rendering or inference.

Deterministic scene assignment (train / test / color split)

$T$, $U$ and $Q$ are not chosen by hand — every downstream formula in this document depends on them, so their origin is a fixed, auditable procedure run before any rendering or inference, with no reference to embeddings:

Train / test splitSort all $s \in S$ by ascending SHA256("gravity-minimal-v1|split|" + s). First 30 IDs → $T$ (train). Last 10 IDs → $U$ (test).
Color-control selectionIndependently sort all $s \in S$ by ascending SHA256("gravity-minimal-v1|color|" + s). First 20 IDs → $Q$ (color controls).

Hash collisions are resolved by ascending scene ID. Both gravity clips of a scene always stay together in the same split. Color embeddings never enter the H3 transport fit. The realized test set is U = [4, 22, 8, 39, 33, 10, 0, 1, 37, 24].

Let $V_s^{B}$, $V_s^{L}$, $V_s^{C}$ denote the baseline, low-gravity and color videos for scene $s$; $V_s^{C}$ exists only for $s \in Q$. Define the fixed sampling operator

$$P(V) = \big(V[0],\, V[10],\, \dots,\, V[150]\big)$$

for each 151-frame, 30 FPS video — these 16 frames cover times 0 to 5 seconds. Let $F$ be the frozen encoder's final normalized token output. With $N = 4608$ and $d = 768$, define:

$$Z_s^{a} = F\big(P(V_s^{a})\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$$
$$z_s^{a} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} Z_s^{a}[n,:] \in \mathbb{R}^{d}, \qquad a \in \{B, L, C\}$$
$$g_s = z_s^{L} - z_s^{B}, \qquad s \in S$$
$$c_s = z_s^{C} - z_s^{B}, \qquad s \in Q$$

The physical intervention sign is always low gravity minus baseline gravity. The appearance sign is always changed color minus baseline color. Mean pooling precedes differencing; no embedding centering or normalization is inserted. Embeddings are stored in float32 and cast to float64 for the calculations below.

For nonzero $a, b \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$:

$$\langle a,b \rangle = \sum_{j=1}^{d} a_j b_j$$
$$\lVert a \rVert_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d} a_j^2}$$
$$\cos(a,b) = \frac{\langle a,b \rangle}{\lVert a \rVert_2 \, \lVert b \rVert_2}$$
Undefined-value rule A vector with norm $\le 10^{-12}$ has an undefined cosine in this protocol. Its ID and magnitude are recorded — its cosine is never replaced with zero, never nudged with an epsilon, and never silently dropped. Only the affected hypothesis is marked unevaluable; all other valid data and calculations stand. All gravity/color vector norms are recorded as diagnostics, not as an additional hypothesis.

2H1 — Gravity–gravity alignment

Hypothesis H1 — directional consistency (Document 1) Gravity intervention vectors tend to align across scenes. Primary quantity: the mean cosine of the 780 distinct gravity-vector pairs. Positive values support positive average alignment; zero or negative values do not. The full distribution must accompany the mean — the sign alone is never read in isolation.

The diagonal of the cosine matrix equals one by construction and is excluded from every summary below.

Gravity cosine matrix$$G_{st} = \cos(g_s,\, g_t), \qquad G \in \mathbb{R}^{40 \times 40}$$
Distinct pairs$$\mathcal{P} = \{(s,t) : 0 \le s < t \le 39\}, \qquad |\mathcal{P}| = \tfrac{40 \cdot 39}{2} = 780$$
Primary H1 quantity$$A = \frac{1}{780}\sum_{(s,t) \in \mathcal{P}} G_{st}$$
Per-scene mean alignment$$a_s = \frac{1}{39}\sum_{t \in S,\, t \ne s} G_{st}$$

Report the 780 values' mean, median, population standard deviation $\sqrt{\text{mean}\big((x-\overline{x})^2\big)}$, min and max; also store all 40 values of $a_s$ — $a_s$ feeds directly into the H2 contrast $\delta_k$ below.

Checking the result
  • $A > 0$ → supports H1 (positive average alignment).
  • $A \le 0$ → does not support H1.
  • The sign of $A$ is never reported alone — the full 780-value spread (mean, median, SD, min, max) must accompany it, since a small positive mean next to a huge spread is weak evidence.
  • This is descriptive only: no p-value or confidence interval is computed for $A$ (see Combined claim & statistical scope).

3H2 — Gravity–color comparison

Hypothesis H2 — relative appearance specificity (Document 1) A scene's gravity vector aligns more with other scenes' gravity vectors than with its own color vector. Primary quantity: the mean of 20 within-scene contrast scores, $D$. Positive values support this specific comparison; nonpositive values do not. This tests one color change (orange→blue at fixed gravity), not all possible appearance transformations.

Order $Q$ numerically as $q_0, \dots, q_{19}$. Define $C \in \mathbb{R}^{40 \times 20}$:

Cross matrix$$C_{sk} = \cos\big(g_s,\, c_{q_k}\big)$$
Same-scene comparison$$m_k = C_{q_k,\,k}$$
Within-scene contrast$$\delta_k = a_{q_k} - m_k$$
Primary H2 quantity$$D = \frac{1}{20}\sum_{k=0}^{19} \delta_k$$

$D > 0$ means each controlled scene's gravity vector is, on average, better aligned with gravity changes elsewhere than with that same scene's own appearance change.

Report all 20 matched cosines $m_k$, all 20 contrasts $\delta_k$, $D$, and the mean/median/population-SD/min/max of the matched cosines. Save the full 800-entry cross matrix and its descriptive summaries as secondary context — the matched primary comparison is never swapped for whichever cross-matrix statistic looks more favorable. The 800 cross-pairs and the 780 gravity pairs reuse the same underlying vectors and are not independent samples.

A color cosine near zero is orthogonality in this representation, not proof of causal independence. A negative value is an oppositely directed change, not simply "more specific." Color and gravity vectors also share a baseline embedding — this dependence is acknowledged, not hidden.
Checking the result
  • $D > 0$ → supports H2 for this specific color control.
  • $D \le 0$ → does not support H2.
  • Report all 20 matched cosines $m_k$ and contrasts $\delta_k$ alongside $D$, plus the full 800-entry cross matrix as secondary context only.
  • The matched primary comparison is never swapped for a more favorable cross-matrix statistic after the fact.

4H3 — 30-train / 10-test transport

Hypothesis H3 — held-out transport (Document 1) Adding the mean training gravity vector improves prediction of held-out low-gravity embeddings over leaving the baseline embedding unchanged. Primary quantity: normalized transport error $R$. $R<1$ supports aggregate improvement; $R \ge 1$ does not. Per-scene errors show whether improvement is widespread or concentrated in a few scenes.

The only fitted object is the mean training displacement:

Fitted training displacement$$d_T = \frac{1}{30}\sum_{s \in T} g_s$$
Held-out prediction$$\hat{z}_s^{L} = z_s^{B} + d_T, \qquad s \in U$$

No regression, learned scale, intercept, whitening, unit-vector averaging, target normalization, or test-set calibration is applied — this estimates both direction and step size directly from the raw training displacements. "Train" here means estimating this one mean vector, not updating any V-JEPA weight.

For each held-out scene:

No-transport (identity) error$$e_{0,s} = \lVert z_s^{B} - z_s^{L} \rVert_2 = \lVert g_s \rVert_2$$
Transport error$$e_{1,s} = \lVert \hat{z}_s^{L} - z_s^{L} \rVert_2 = \lVert d_T - g_s \rVert_2$$
Individual error ratio$$r_s = \frac{e_{1,s}}{e_{0,s}}$$
Held-out direction alignment$$h_s = \cos(d_T, g_s)$$

Aggregate quantities:

$$\mathrm{RMSE}_0 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{10}\sum_{s \in U} e_{0,s}^{2}}$$
$$\mathrm{RMSE}_1 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{10}\sum_{s \in U} e_{1,s}^{2}}$$
Primary H3 quantity$$R = \frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_1}{\mathrm{RMSE}_0}$$
$$\text{improvement} = 1 - R$$
$$f = \frac{1}{10}\sum_{s \in U} \mathbb{1}\big[e_{1,s} < e_{0,s}\big]$$
$$H = \frac{1}{10}\sum_{s \in U} h_s$$
Value of $R$Interpretation
$R < 1$Aggregate improvement — transported prediction beats the naive baseline
$R = 1$No aggregate improvement
$R > 1$Transport is worse than doing nothing
$R = 0$Perfect prediction

Report $R$, both RMSEs, $f$, $H$, $\lVert d_T \rVert_2$, and all ten rows of $e_0, e_1, r, h$. $R$ is never confused with the mean of the individual ratios $r_s$, and Euclidean errors are never called squared errors.

Undefined cases If $e_{0,s} \le 10^{-12}$, then $r_s$ is undefined and must be marked accordingly. If $\lVert d_T \rVert_2 \le 10^{-12}$, directional cosines $h_s$ are undefined, although the additive predictions and absolute errors still exist. If $\mathrm{RMSE}_0 \le 10^{-12}$, $R$ is undefined and H3 cannot be evaluated as planned. No replacement split is allowed in any of these cases.
Checking the result
  • $R < 1$ → supports H3 (aggregate improvement over the naive baseline).
  • $R \ge 1$ → does not support H3.
  • Read $f$ (fraction of the 10 held-out scenes individually improved) and $H$ (mean held-out alignment with $d_T$) alongside $R$ — a favorable $R$ driven by one or two scenes, with a low $f$, is a materially weaker result than broad improvement across most held-out scenes.

5Combined claim & statistical scope

H1, H2 and H3 are three separate descriptive hypotheses, each checked against its own criterion above. A limited combined claim — "average alignment, separation from this color control, and transport improvement in this fixed benchmark" — only holds if all three meet their directional criteria. Mixed outcomes (for example, H1 and H3 support while H2 does not) are reported as mixed, never folded into a single verdict.

This is not a significance test The 40 scenes are a designed grid, not an independent random sample from real videos. No confirmatory population p-values or confidence intervals are computed for $A$, $D$ or $R$. No threshold may be changed after seeing the embeddings, and no split, metric or condition may be revised because a result looked unfavorable. Small positive differences ($A$ or $D$ just above 0; $R$ just below 1) are weak evidence, not confirmation — crossing zero or crossing one is a descriptive threshold, not a statistical significance claim. This is a deliberately small initial experiment meant to characterize effect sizes and feasibility, not to confirm a population-level effect.

6Expected outputs and plots

  1. Embedding table — 100 rows × 768 values, with scene and condition identifiers and video hashes.
  2. Vector sets — gravity vectors ($40 \times 768$); color vectors ($20 \times 768$); gravity cosine matrix ($40 \times 40$); gravity–color cosine matrix ($40 \times 20$).
  3. Matched-control table — scene ID, $a_s$, matched color cosine, $\delta$, and vector norms.
  4. Transport table — the ten scene IDs, $e_0, e_1, r, h$, plus a separately saved training mean and predicted embeddings.
  5. Summary JSON — $A$, $D$, $R$, and all prespecified descriptive summaries, with undefined quantities explicitly represented and explained.
  6. Gravity heatmap — fixed color scale $[-1,1]$, all scene IDs, diagonal shown but excluded from statistics.
  7. Gravity–color heatmap — fixed scale $[-1,1]$, rows and columns labeled by their actual scene IDs.
  8. Cosine histogram — 30 equal bins with edges $-1 + \tfrac{2k}{30}$ for $k = 0,\dots,30$; separately normalized densities for the 780 gravity pairs and the 20 matched color pairs, noting unequal sample counts and dependence.
  9. Matched contrast plot — 20 $\delta$ values against scene ID with a zero line.
  10. Transport plot — paired $e_0$/$e_1$ bars for each test ID, annotated with the aggregate $R$. No error bars implying independent pair sampling.
Cosines are clamped to $[-1,1]$ only for tiny floating-point boundary overshoot, and only after verifying the excess is $< 10^{-10}$ — a larger excess is treated as an implementation error, not a clamp. Original unclamped values are kept in the audit data.

7Integrity checks

Before trusting any experimental number, the implementation verifies: sample counts and unique IDs; disjoint train/test sets; matching physical metadata; exact RGB round-trip; finite $[4608,768]$ token arrays and finite 768-vectors; expected cosine symmetry and diagonal within $10^{-10}$; and the identities $e_1 = \lVert d_T - g_s \rVert$ and $e_0 = \lVert g_s \rVert$. The analysis code is validated on small, hand-computable vectors before it ever touches experimental embeddings — these software checks are not additional experimental conditions.

$d_T$ is computed using only the recorded $T$ IDs and saved before any test metric is produced, then recomputed from the saved training vectors to verify provenance. If any implementation defect is discovered after inference, the defect, the correction and the affected outputs are all documented. A scientific design choice is never silently changed to improve results.

§Appendix — stimulus-generation equations

Everything above analyzes embeddings after the 100 clips exist. These equations generate the clips themselves (Document 1). They are a different category — rendering physics, not representation analysis — but are included here so every calculation in the experiment lives in one place.

Scene grid (i = 0..7, j = 0..4)$$s = 8j + i, \qquad x_{0,s} = -70 + 10i \text{ m}, \qquad y_{0,s} = 140 + 5j \text{ m}$$
Ballistic trajectory, t ∈ [0, 5] s, with $v_x{=}15$, $v_{y0}{=}10$$$x_s(t) = x_{0,s} + 15t, \qquad y_{s,g}(t) = y_{0,s} + 10t - \tfrac{g}{2}t^2$$
Camera projection (world → 384×384 px)$$u = 1.92(x + 100), \qquad v = 1.92(200 - y), \qquad r_{\text{px}} = 9.6$$

design_revision ballistic-launch-v3: the original $v_x{=}2$, $v_{y0}{=}0$ setup (near-vertical horizontal launch) visually read as a weak, unresolved drop. The ball now launches at an angle ($v_{y0}{=}10$ m/s upward), giving a standard rise-then-fall ballistic parabola instead. The $x_0$ grid spacing tightened from 20 m to 10 m to keep the larger horizontal travel (75 m over 5s, up from 10 m) inside the world. World $x \in [-100,100]$ m, $y \in [0,200]$ m; ball radius $r=5$ m. Numerically verified in-bounds for all 40 scenes at both $g=9.8$ and $g=4.9$: $x \in [-70,75]$, $y \in [67.5,170.2]$ (margins $x\in(-95,95)$, $y\in(5,195)$). Background RGB $(32,32,32)$, grid RGB $(44,44,44)$. Orange $(235,100,60)$ for both gravity conditions; blue $(60,140,235)$ for the 20 color controls.
