2Control-model baseline

The obvious objection to the headline numbers: maybe any reasonable video representation shows this, simply because a gravity change alters the visible trajectory, and trajectory differences are a generically strong signal in any representation. To test that directly, the identical locked analysis code (same 780 pairs, same 20 matched scenes, same 30/10 split) was run on the exact same 100 videos through two control representations.

Both controls collapse to chance — or worse than the naive baseline — on every hypothesis. The random-weight architecture is the stronger control: it is the exact same ViT-B/16, patch embedding, mean-pooling, and cosine math as the real result. The only difference is whether the weights were ever pretrained. Its failure rules out "this is just a generic property of the architecture/geometry." The pixel-statistics baseline (no model at all) rules out "this is just a generic property of how the scene visually changes." Neither reproduces any part of the signal.

This does not extend the claim beyond what's stated in §1 — still one checkpoint, one renderer, no causal disentanglement from trajectory shape (see Limitations). It addresses a different question: whether the signal is specific to the trained model at all, or an artifact of the experimental setup. It is specific to the trained model.

3Putting this in perspective — what it says about V-JEPA's claims

V-JEPA's central architectural bet — restated in the V-JEPA2 paper's own title, "Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning" — is that training a model to predict in latent space, rather than reconstructing pixels, forces it to discard irrelevant texture/noise and retain the physically/semantically meaningful structure of a scene, without labels. This experiment's numbers map onto three separable pieces of that claim, plus one check on what actually produced them.

What this does not test V-JEPA2's own headline "prediction and planning" results (robot-arm planning, next-state forecasting) exercise a different component: the trained predictor network, which forecasts masked/future latent states from partial context. This experiment only ever ran the frozen encoder on complete, already-rendered clips — the predictor module was never invoked. This result is evidence the encoder learns a structured, physically-relevant, generalizable representation — a necessary precondition for the predictor to have something useful to forecast — not a direct replication of the forward-simulation/planning claim itself.

Reading the magnitudes honestly. None of these numbers are saturated. A=0.635 and D=0.598 sit well below the 1.0 ceiling — there is real per-scene variability, not one perfectly crisp shared axis. R=0.601 still leaves substantial residual error (RMSE₁=0.419, not 0) — a 40% reduction, not a solved prediction problem. The honest summary: this encoder's latent space is usefully and non-trivially structured around this physical variable — not perfectly or deterministically so — and that structure demonstrably required V-JEPA2's actual pretraining, not just its architecture.

4Methodology

40 scenes, each a ball launched on a ballistic trajectory (x(t)=x0+15t, y(t)=y0+10t−0.5·g·t²), rendered twice — once at g=9.8 m/s², once at g=4.9 m/s² — with every other pixel-level factor (position, velocity, color, camera, background) held fixed. 20 of those 40 scenes additionally get a color-control clip (orange→blue ball, gravity fixed at 9.8). 100 videos total, each 151 frames @ 30 FPS (5 seconds), rendered as lossless FFV1.

Each video → frozen V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B/16 EMA encoder (strict-loaded, zero missing/unexpected keys, checkpoint SHA-256 848a77c3...59ddf4d) → 16 sampled frames → final-layer tokens [1,4608,768] → mean-pooled to one 768-d vector. Gravity vector g_s = z(s,4.9) − z(s,9.8); color vector c_s = z(s,color) − z(s,9.8).

Executed on a RunPod RTX 3090: render 68s, extract 49s, analyze <1s — full 100-clip pipeline in ~2 minutes of compute. The two control baselines added ~3 minutes more (random-weight forward passes + trivial pixel stats).

8Combined claim

H1, H2, and H3 are three separate descriptive hypotheses; all three independently met their directional criteria, so the limited combined claim in §1 holds. Had any one failed, this would be reported as a mixed outcome, not folded into a single verdict — that didn't happen here, but the rule is stated because it's the rule that would have applied.

9Implementation deviations

One deviation, made before execution and fully documented in design/01_EXPERIMENT_DESIGN.md and scene_plan.json (design_revision: ballistic-launch-v3): the initial physics draft (vx=2, vy0=0) produced a visually weak, near-vertical drop rather than a recognizable projectile arc. This was replaced with a proper ballistic launch (vx=15, vy0=10) before any rendering or inference — verified numerically in-bounds for all 40 scenes × both gravity values × all 151 frames before use. No other deviation occurred: the executed pipeline matches the frozen math plan exactly.

10Limitations

Stated plainly, not buried
  • Descriptive, not inferential. The 40 scenes are a designed grid, not a random sample. No p-values or confidence intervals anywhere; A>0, D>0, R<1 are descriptive thresholds, not significance claims.
  • One synthetic renderer, one checkpoint. Says nothing about real-world footage, a different renderer, or a different V-JEPA scale — the control baseline rules out "any representation," not "any checkpoint of V-JEPA."
  • No causal disentanglement from trajectory shape. Gravity necessarily changes the visible trajectory here; this cannot separate "encodes an abstract gravity variable" from "encodes the resulting motion shape."
  • One color control, not an exhaustive appearance-confound sweep, and it also changes luminance.
  • All three hypotheses are separate; the combined claim only holds because all three independently passed.
